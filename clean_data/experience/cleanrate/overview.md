# Cleanrate — Overview

## Summary
Cleanrate was an early-stage Columbia, SC startup trying to eliminate in-person home energy inspections. I worked there as a Software Engineer for ~2.5 months (Dec 2024 – Feb 2025, ~1.5 months of active part-time work), unpaid, fully remote and fully self-directed, reporting to the Lead Software Engineer. I built the proof-of-concept HPXML prototype.

## Context
In the US, homeowners can get tax and insurance discounts if their home meets a certain energy-efficiency standard, measured via a Home Energy Score (HPXML). Normally that requires a specialist to physically inspect the home. Cleanrate's product idea was to replace the specialist with software: the homeowner submits house specs through a form and gets an instant energy score. My job was to prove the core concept was technically feasible.

The startup was later shut down due to a change in government regulations that undermined the core business model (the exact regulatory change is unknown to me).

## Role
- **Title:** Software Engineer
- **Duration:** ~2.5 months (Dec 2024 – Feb 2025); ~1.5 months of active work, part-time
- **Compensation:** unpaid
- **Type:** informal, self-directed opportunity — not a formal internship. No structured program, no onboarding, no timeline given.

## Team Structure
3 software engineers total: a Lead Software Engineer (who I reported to), one additional Software Engineer, and me.

## Work Style
Fully remote, fully self-directed. No guidance, no onboarding, no deadlines — given a task and expected to deliver independently.

## Results
Delivered a working client-side prototype that generated a valid HPXML document accepted by the official US government Home Energy Score validator — the technical proof-of-concept for Cleanrate's entire product. (Detail in the hpxml_prototype file.)

## Lessons Learned
This role was a lesson in operating with zero scaffolding — no onboarding, no deadlines, poor documentation — and still delivering a validated technical result. The business outcome (shutdown) was outside engineering control; the engineering deliverable succeeded.
