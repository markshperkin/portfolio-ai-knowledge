# University of South Carolina (Teaching Assistant) — Overview

## Summary
I was a Teaching Assistant at USC for ~9 months (August 2024 – May 2025, 2 semesters), the sole TA for 3 distinct courses across 100+ students, working ~20 hours/week — while simultaneously a full-time M.S. Computer Science student carrying 12–13 credits of 700-level graduate coursework. The TA position also funded my degree.

## Context
As an international student, USC tuition was prohibitively expensive. The TA role was a deliberate strategy: it funded the M.S., and without it continuing the degree wouldn't have been financially feasible. So the role had a dual purpose — professional development and making the degree itself possible. It was real financial pressure, not just a resume line.

## Role
- **Title:** Teaching Assistant
- **Duration:** ~9 months / 2 semesters (Aug 2024 – May 2025)
- **Location:** Columbia, SC
- **Time commitment:** ~20 hours/week

100+ students across all courses. I was the sole TA per course — no other TAs.

## Responsibilities
- Weekly lab sessions (open Q&A / office-hours style, supplementary — not mandatory).
- Grading code assignments, reports, and papers.
- Mentoring students through implementation, debugging, and algorithmic trade-off analysis.
- Built and maintained an automated grading pipeline.

## Context That Shaped It
I did this as a concurrent full-time M.S. student carrying 12–13 credits of 700-level graduate coursework each semester. Having been a Division I student-athlete for 5 years, balancing a heavy academic load with a major time commitment was already familiar territory.

## Results
Supported 3 distinct courses solo across 2 semesters for 100+ students, built a grading pipeline that cut grading time by 90%+, and funded the M.S. degree in the process. (Detail in the other usc files.)

## Lessons Learned
The role only worked because I engineered around its biggest time sink (the grading pipeline) instead of grinding through it manually — that's the same constrained-resource instinct from the student-athlete years, applied to a job.
