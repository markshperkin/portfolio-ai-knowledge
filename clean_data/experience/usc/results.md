# USC (TA) — Results

## Summary
As sole TA for 3 courses across 2 semesters and 100+ students, I cut grading time by 90%+ with an automated pipeline, drove measurable student improvement through lab support and feedback, deepened my own AI knowledge by teaching it, and funded my M.S. degree in the process.

## Grading Efficiency
- Grading time reduced from 10–20 hours/week → 1–2 hours/week.
- 90%+ reduction in manual evaluation time.
- 200+ weekly submissions processed through the automated pipeline.

## Student Outcomes
- Clear correlation between lab attendance and grades: consistent attendees earned A's; non-attendees often received C's or failed.
- Students who received written feedback on mistakes demonstrably improved on subsequent submissions.
- Lab sessions made a measurably difficult curriculum more accessible — the courses were hard enough that supplementary support had real impact.

## Course Coverage
Supported 3 distinct courses across 2 semesters as the sole TA:
- Programming Languages & Structures (Java-focused)
- Artificial Intelligence (full AI breadth: ML, deep learning, RL, classical AI)
- Algorithmic Design II (algorithms and data structures)

## Professional & Personal Growth
- Significant growth in mentoring and teaching — translating complex CS concepts into accessible explanations.
- Sharpened Java and Python through hands-on grading and lab support.
- Deepened AI knowledge by teaching it — reinforced material I'd already studied in depth as a student through repeated explanation and student questions.
- Expanded my professional network with USC faculty (James O'Reilly, Jeremiah Shepherd).
- Developed academic-integrity judgment — reported violations despite personal discomfort, prioritizing fairness.

## Financial
The TA position funded the M.S. Computer Science degree — it made continued enrollment financially viable as an international student. This was a primary, intended outcome of the role, not a side benefit.

## Lessons Learned
The role produced a clean teaching signal: attendance and feedback measurably moved grades, which is the kind of evidence I trust. And the most valuable personal result was indirect — teaching AI made me better at AI than studying it alone did.
