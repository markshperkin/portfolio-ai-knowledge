# USC — Teaching, Labs & Mentoring

## Summary
Beyond the grading pipeline, the core of the TA role was teaching: running weekly lab sessions for three courses (Programming Languages & Structures, Artificial Intelligence, Algorithmic Design II), giving detailed written feedback, and mentoring students through implementation, debugging, and algorithmic trade-offs.

## Lab Sessions — Programming Languages & Structures (Fall 2024)
- **Format:** open Q&A / office hours, supplementary (not mandatory).
- **Language focus:** Java.
- **Topics:** Java code behavior, data structures, algorithms, debugging, implementation patterns.
- **Approach:** whiteboard explanations, live walkthroughs of student code, guided debugging.

## Lab Sessions — Artificial Intelligence (Spring 2025)
- **Format:** open Q&A / office hours.
- **Topics:** classical AI algorithms (A*, search); classical ML algorithms; neural network architectures (MLP, CNNs, RNNs, LSTM, Transformers); reinforcement learning (on-policy vs. off-policy, game theory); general trade-off analysis across architectures and use cases.
- **Depth:** this was a breadth-first course — it covered the full AI umbrella at a conceptual/trade-off level rather than deep implementation dives. Lab sessions reinforced *when to use what and why*.
- **Hardest topic for students:** reinforcement learning (on-policy vs. off-policy, game theory) — addressed mostly through verbal explanation and whiteboard work.

## Lab Sessions — Algorithmic Design II (Spring 2025)
- **Format:** open Q&A / office hours.
- **Language focus:** Java and Python.
- **Topics:** advanced algorithms, data structures, algorithmic trade-off analysis, implementation and debugging.

## Grading & Feedback (All Courses)
- Graded code assignments, written reports, and papers.
- Wrote detailed comments on mistakes in submissions.
- Tracked improvement across submissions — students who received feedback consistently improved on later work.
- Applied a strict rubric; reported academic-integrity violations when identified.

## Results
- Clear correlation between lab attendance and grades: consistent attendees earned A's; non-attendees often got C's or failed.
- Students who received written feedback on mistakes demonstrably improved on subsequent submissions.
- Lab sessions made a measurably difficult curriculum more accessible — the courses were hard enough that supplementary support had real impact.

## Lessons Learned
Teaching AI deepened my own understanding of it — explaining MLPs, CNNs, RNNs, Transformers, and RL trade-offs repeatedly, and fielding student questions, reinforced material I'd already studied as a student. The hardest thing to teach (RL intuition) is also the thing I now understand best, precisely because I had to make it land verbally.
