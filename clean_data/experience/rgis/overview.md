# RGIS — Overview

## Summary
I worked as an Applied AI Engineer at RGIS for 3 months (February 1 – April 30, 2026), full-time, as the sole developer on a 2-person AI team. I owned all technical development end-to-end while the AI Director handled stakeholders. The flagship deliverable was Leads Finder, an end-to-end agentic lead-intelligence pipeline.

## Context
RGIS is a global inventory, retail optimization, and supply-chain services company — physical inventory counting, retail merchandising and store audits, warehouse and supply-chain optimization, asset tracking, data collection, and operational analytics for enterprise clients. The business runs on large-scale operational data collection and decision-making. During my time there, RGIS was actively shifting from a manual operational-services company toward an AI-assisted operational intelligence and automation company. I was hired into that transition.

## Role
- **Title:** Applied AI Engineer
- **Type:** Full-time
- **Duration:** 3 months (Feb 1 – Apr 30, 2026)

## Team Structure
A 2-person AI team:
- **Me** — sole developer, owned all technical development end-to-end.
- **AI Director** — my direct manager; owned stakeholder communication, requirements gathering, resource provisioning, and review of deliverables.

No other engineers on the AI team. I reported directly to the AI Director.

## Context That Shaped the Engagement
I worked through this entire role during a 5-week international relocation (Israel → United States) in the middle of an active war, with repeated flight cancellations. I held a 10–11 hour/day schedule including weekends throughout, delivered twice-weekly production releases, and operated across a large time-zone gap (11am Israel = 3am US East Coast for stakeholder meetings). Because of that gap, the Director took the stakeholder-facing meetings and I handled all development plus the indirect translation of requirements.

## Results
Built RGIS's first automated lead-intelligence system from scratch as sole developer, shipped twice weekly for 3 months, and delivered it through significant personal and operational disruption. (Metrics in the results file.)

## Lessons Learned
The biggest professional lesson: not being in the stakeholder meetings meant second-hand requirements and solutions that were correct but not perfectly tailored. That experience is exactly why I now target Forward Deployed Engineer roles.
