# Personal Background

## Summary
I'm Mark Shperkin — Israeli, Bukharian Jewish, born and raised in Jerusalem. I'm the older of two (one younger sister), the child of two highly-educated Soviet immigrants, and I'm married to an American I met on my first day of class at the University of South Carolina. This file is the human context behind everything else in the corpus — where I'm from, who my people are, where I live now.

## Context
Most of what's documented elsewhere (athletics, education, work) makes more sense once you know the household I grew up in: Soviet-immigrant, education-obsessed, no-excuses, deeply Bukharian on the food and culture side. The "athlete mentality" I talk about professionally didn't appear out of nowhere — it was the operating system of the house long before I picked up swimming.

## Where I'm From
- **Born and raised in Jerusalem.** Lived there for the majority of my life.
- **Ages 19–21 — Netanya.** Moved to Netanya to train at the Wingate Institute and Israel's national pool. That's where the serious competitive swimming years happened in Israel, before USC.
- **Currently — Israel**, with my wife. Planning to start our family here.

## Family
- **Younger sister** — one sibling.
- **Parents — former USSR.** My mom is from Uzbekistan, my dad is from Ukraine. Both immigrated to Israel when the USSR fell, and they met each other in Hebrew school in Israel.
- **Both highly educated, both with master's degrees.** Dad has a B.S. and M.S. in Mathematics. Mom has a B.S. and M.S. in Pharmacy. Education in our house wasn't aspirational — it was the floor.
- **Russian was the home language** growing up, which is why I'm fluent in Russian today on top of Hebrew and English.

## Wife
I met my wife at USC, in my **first class** there. She's American, a **registered nurse**, and we got married in **August 2025**. The move to Israel after the wedding has been hard for her — life here is different from America, for good and bad. We're mostly happy here and looking to build our family in Israel.

People always ask me which is better, Israel or the US, and I genuinely can't say — both are good and bad for their own reasons. I could see myself raising a family in either country.

## The Soviet Household
The Soviet-immigrant upbringing shows up clearly in how I was raised and how I work today:
- Education emphasis — non-negotiable
- Excellence pressure — the standard was always high
- Strong work ethic — built into the air of the house
- No excuses — outcomes were owned, not explained away
- Resilience — Soviets are tough people, and that toughness was passed down

A lot of what I bring to engineering — long-horizon discipline, no-excuses ownership — was already baked into the household before I ever touched a pool or a keyboard.
