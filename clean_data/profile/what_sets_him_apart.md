# What Sets Me Apart

## Summary
Technical skills are table stakes — most candidates for the roles I want can write the code. My differentiator is the athlete's mentality built over 20 years of Division I, SEC-level competitive swimming. It's the same mindset that got me to SEC B-finals and 35 hundredths from an Olympic relay, and I bring it to every engineering problem.

## Context
I spent 20 years (age 6–26) in competitive swimming, finishing with 5 seasons as a Division I SEC athlete at USC while earning a CS degree. That environment — not a bootcamp, not a side project — is where my professional traits actually formed. (See the athletics files for the full story; this file is the professional positioning version.)

## The Differentiators
- **Dedication** — sustained high output over long periods, with no shortcuts. Twenty years of twice-daily practices with no off-seasons is the baseline I measure effort against.
- **Motivation** — self-driven. I don't need external pressure to perform; the standard is internal.
- **Work ethic** — built in an environment where 4:45am wake-ups and two practices a day were normal, not exceptional.
- **Time management** — I ran a D1 athletic career, a rigorous CS degree, and relationships at the same time, with no margin for waste. Constrained-resource scheduling is second nature.
- **"Zero regrets" attitude** — I put 100% into everything so that outcomes, good or bad, are owned without second-guessing. It's why I could walk away from a near-Olympic shot cleanly and why I commit fully to engineering problems.

## Why This Matters Professionally
The roles I'm targeting (AI Engineer, Algorithm Engineer, Forward Deployed Engineer) reward exactly these traits: long-horizon focus, self-direction, performing under pressure, and full ownership of outcomes. The competitive mindset isn't a nice anecdote — it's the operating system I run engineering work on.

## Lessons Learned
Skill gets you considered; mentality gets you through the hard parts of real projects. I lead with the technical work, but the reason I deliver under pressure and ambiguity is the 20 years behind it.
