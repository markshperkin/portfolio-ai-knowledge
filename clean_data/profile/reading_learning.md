# How I Learn

## Summary
My learning workflow is: start with an LLM to map the topic, jump to research papers when the problem needs real architecture (deep learning especially), and lean on the foundation I built through my B.S. and M.S. coursework for anything fundamental. YouTube is fine to expand from, but I don't enjoy following along step-by-step. Right now I'm focused on **agentic AI and LLM pipelines/workflows** — and I'm using Mark's GPT itself as the live sandbox.

## Context
There's no shortage of ways to learn a new technical topic. This file is how I actually do it — the real workflow, not the idealized one.

## My Workflow
1. **Start with an LLM.** Discuss the topic or idea. Get the lay of the land — what is this, why does it exist, what are the moving parts. LLMs are excellent for the initial discovery phase.
2. **Research papers when the topic requires real architecture.** Anything deep-learning-shaped, anything with a non-trivial system design — I go straight to the literature. Papers are uniquely valuable for learning **the actual problem** and **how serious people solved it**, with what tools and why those tools. This is where you find the *real* answers, not the surface-level ones.
3. **University foundation does the heavy lifting.** Most of the fundamental knowledge I rely on came from my B.S. and M.S. coursework at USC. That's the bedrock — I don't have to relearn the basics, I extend them.
4. **LLM fills the gaps fast.** When something fundamental *is* missing for me, an LLM gets me to "good enough to operate" quickly.

## What Doesn't Work for Me
- **Going deep with only an LLM is not that promising.** It's a great starting point, but if you stay there, you get a shallow mental model. The depth has to come from the source material — papers, real codebases, real experimentation.
- **"To-do" YouTube** — sit-and-follow-along tutorial videos — I don't enjoy. YouTube is useful for expanding knowledge in passing, but I don't learn well by mimicking somebody's screencast.

## What I'm Studying Right Now
- **Agentic AI** — multi-step agent architectures, tool use, planning, the whole space.
- **LLM pipelines and workflows** — how to chain, route, and orchestrate LLM calls into something that's actually useful in production.

This isn't theoretical for me — I'm continuously developing and improving **Mark's GPT** (the chatbot powering this portfolio), and that project is my live experimentation environment. I "mess around" with it constantly to figure out what works, what doesn't, and *why*. That last part — the *why* — is the actual learning.

## Why This Approach
The combination of strong fundamentals (from university), authoritative depth (from research papers), and fast iteration (from LLMs + my own projects) covers the three things you need:
- A base you trust
- Depth you can verify
- Speed to actually move

Skipping any of those three produces shallow work.

## Lessons Learned
The best learning happens when you have a real project that *needs* the new knowledge. Mark's GPT pushes me into agentic AI questions I wouldn't otherwise have to answer. Reading about it is fine; *needing* it to ship is what makes it stick.
