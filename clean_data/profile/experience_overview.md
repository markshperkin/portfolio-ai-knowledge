# Professional Experience Overview

## Summary
This is the high-level picture of my work experience — the jobs I've held, what I did in each, and how they fit together. I've held three professional roles: Applied AI Engineer at RGIS, Software Engineer at Cleanrate, and Teaching Assistant at the University of South Carolina. Across those roles my work experience spans production AI engineering, building a technical proof-of-concept from scratch, and running graduate-level CS courses solo. If you want to know what experience I have, what jobs I've worked, or what my career background looks like — this is the summary, and each role has its own detailed files.

## Context
My professional experience is early-career but deliberately broad: a full-time applied AI engineering job, a self-directed software engineering role at a startup, and a sustained teaching/engineering role at a university. None of these were passive resume lines — each had real stakes (a flagship deliverable, a company's core technical bet, a degree that the job itself funded). Taken together they cover shipping production AI, proving technical feasibility under ambiguity, and owning systems end-to-end.

## Technical Details
My work history, most recent first:

- **RGIS — Applied AI Engineer (full-time, 3 months, Feb 1 – Apr 30, 2026).** Sole developer on a 2-person AI team; owned all technical development end-to-end. Built RGIS's first automated lead-intelligence system, *Leads Finder*, an end-to-end agentic pipeline, shipped twice weekly. This is my most substantial professional engineering experience.
- **Cleanrate — Software Engineer (~2.5 months, Dec 2024 – Feb 2025, unpaid, self-directed).** Built the proof-of-concept HPXML prototype that was the technical bet for the whole product — a client-side prototype generating a valid HPXML document accepted by the official US government Home Energy Score validator. Fully remote, zero scaffolding.
- **USC — Teaching Assistant (~9 months, Aug 2024 – May 2025, ~20 h/week).** Sole TA for 3 distinct graduate CS courses across 100+ students while a full-time M.S. student; built an automated grading pipeline that cut grading time 90%+. This job also funded the M.S. degree.

## Challenges
Each role carried a real constraint: I delivered the RGIS job through a 5-week wartime international relocation with repeated flight cancellations and an 11-hour stakeholder time-zone gap; Cleanrate had no onboarding, no deadlines, and no guidance; the USC role ran concurrently with 12–13 credits of 700-level graduate coursework.

## Solutions
The common thread across my experience is engineering around the binding constraint instead of grinding through it: an agentic pipeline at RGIS shipped on a punishing schedule, a validated prototype at Cleanrate built with no scaffolding, and an automated grading pipeline at USC that made a solo-TA load across three courses sustainable.

## Results
- RGIS: built and shipped the company's first automated lead-intelligence system as sole developer, twice-weekly releases for 3 months.
- Cleanrate: delivered the validated technical proof-of-concept for the company's entire product.
- USC: supported 3 courses solo for 100+ students, cut grading time 90%+, and funded the M.S. degree.

## Lessons Learned
The biggest professional lesson came from RGIS: receiving requirements second-hand produced solutions that were correct but not perfectly tailored — which is exactly why I now target Forward Deployed Engineer roles. Across all three jobs the pattern is the same: constrained resources, ownership, and engineering the way around the bottleneck.
