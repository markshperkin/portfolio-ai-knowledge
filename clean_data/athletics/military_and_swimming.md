# Military Service & Swimming

## Summary
I served three years in the Israeli Air Force (2016–2019), mandatory service for Israeli citizens, and managed to keep swimming at a competitive level the entire time by earning formal "Elite Athlete" status within the military.

## Context
After high school I went straight into mandatory service in the Israeli Air Force. For most athletes, three years of military service is where a competitive swimming career quietly ends — the schedule and demands don't leave room for twice-daily training. I didn't want that to happen.

## Technical Details
I earned **'Elite Athlete' status** within the military — a formal designation that permitted me to:
- Continue competitive training during service.
- Travel for international competitions.
- Participate in international training camps.

This wasn't automatic. It's a recognized status the military grants to athletes performing at a high enough level, and it's what allowed me to stay in the sport through those three years instead of losing them.

## Challenges
The Air Force is demanding and structured around the military's needs, not an athlete's training plan. Maintaining competitive-level swimming on top of service obligations meant constantly fitting training into whatever space the schedule allowed, and traveling for competition while still meeting my duties.

## Solutions
The Elite Athlete designation gave me the formal permission structure; the discipline from the military environment is what actually made it work. The high-discipline environment translated directly — it's the same muscle I later used to balance a Division I athletic career with a CS degree.

## Results
I maintained competitive-level swimming throughout all three years of service and earned an **honorable discharge as First Sergeant**. The military years didn't interrupt my career — I came out of them and went straight into Division I college swimming at USC.

## Lessons Learned
Structure and constraint aren't the enemy of high performance — they can be the thing that makes it possible. The military discipline didn't slow my swimming down; it became the backbone for everything I balanced afterward, including the student-athlete years.
