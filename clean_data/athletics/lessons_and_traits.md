# What Swimming Gave Me — Hardest Moments and Professional Traits

## Summary
Twenty years of competitive swimming is where my professional traits actually come from: dedication, working under pressure, time management, resilience, and the "zero regrets" philosophy. This file covers the hardest moments and the traits they built.

## The Hardest Moments

**2013 — broken hand, age 15.** Three months before nationals I broke my hand. For two months I could only kick — no arm training at all. I assumed the season was over. At nationals I won my first medal: 2nd place. The lesson stuck hard: consistent effort, even under a serious constraint, still produces results. "No matter what, I just have to keep pushing."

**Student-athlete relationship strain.** During the USC years, swimming left almost no time for my relationship with my girlfriend (now my wife). The accumulated sacrifice on both sides was real, and it became part of the retirement calculus in 2024.

## The "Zero Regrets" Philosophy
The single core lesson from 20 years of competitive swimming: put 100% into everything, so that when you fail you know you did your best. No second-guessing. This governs how I approach engineering work, career decisions, and life. It's why I could walk away from a near-Olympic shot without regret — I'd given it everything, so the decision was clean.

## Professional Traits, Directly From Swimming
- **Dedication** — 20 years of twice-daily practices, no off-seasons, no exceptions.
- **Goal setting and timelines** — every season had a target time, a target meet, a target place. I think in measurable targets by default.
- **Working under pressure** — SEC finals, international meets, a broken hand at nationals. High-stakes performance is familiar territory.
- **Time management** — managed a D1 athletic career, a CS degree, relationships, and recovery simultaneously with no margin for waste.
- **Leadership** — self-initiated team leadership with no title and no requirement; led because it was needed.
- **Resilience** — broken hand → medal; "always 17th" → qualified at 16th; military → continued training; near-Olympic shot → retired with zero regrets.

## Results
The same competitive mindset that got me to SEC B-finals and 35 hundredths from an Olympic relay is the one I bring to every engineering problem. Technical skill is table stakes; this is the differentiator.

## Lessons Learned
Constraints don't excuse you from the result — the broken hand still ended in a medal. And the only way to make a hard decision cleanly is to have genuinely given the thing your full effort beforehand.

## Swimming Today
The competitive chapter is closed. I still swim 1–2 times a week, 20–30 minutes, for fun and health, and I still watch competitions. The relationship with the sport continues; the competition part is done.
