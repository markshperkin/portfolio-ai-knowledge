# Education — Challenges

## Summary
The hard parts of my education weren't abstract. They were concrete: a Division I athletic schedule layered on a CS degree, starting university at 21 after the military, failing and recovering Calculus II, funding the M.S. as an international student, compressing a thesis into 2 months, and a research project that failed at the hardware stage and had to pivot.

## 1. Student-Athlete Schedule
Division I SEC swimming is effectively a second full-time job: two-a-day practices (6–8am + 3–5pm), daily gym, physical therapy, nutrition, sleep management — ~30+ hours a week — on top of a heavy CS degree. Wake at 4:45am. Every other weekend was a dual meet; SEC Championships meant a full week away with coursework finished in advance. Resolution: exceptional time management — no wasted gaps, quality academic work produced under physical fatigue.

## 2. Starting University at 21 (Post-Military)
I began the B.S. at 21 after 3 years in the Israeli Air Force — older than classmates, with a gap in academic momentum, adapting to US university culture at the same time. The military's high-discipline environment ultimately helped more than the age gap hurt; the structure and resilience transferred directly to the student-athlete load.

## 3. Calculus II Failure and Recovery
Failed Calculus II in Fall 2021 (hard course, compounded by the athletic schedule). Retook it Spring 2022 and earned an A; grade forgiveness applied. Concrete proof of identifying a weakness and fixing it rather than excusing it.

## 4. Financial Pressure (M.S.)
As an international student, USC tuition was cost-prohibitive for the M.S. without income. The TA position wasn't just professional development — it was financially necessary to stay enrolled. I secured it, and it funded the degree. (Detail in experience/usc.)

## 5. Completing a Thesis in 2 Months
USC's standard M.S. thesis timeline is ~2 years. I did mine in one summer (~2 months) while wrapping up the degree. Resolution: dedicated Summer 2025 entirely to it and delivered a published, committee-approved thesis on time.

## 6. Hardware Research Failure (Directed Study)
The CSCE 798 directed study started as a custom wearable IMU suit; sensor noise and drift made the data unusable and forced a full mid-project pivot. I recognized the dead end, pivoted to ST-GCN on NTU RGB+D, hit 87.6% accuracy, and documented the failure honestly in the paper.

## 7. Balancing CS Rigor with Athletics
CS at USC is math-heavy (Calc I/II, Vector Calculus, Linear Algebra, Discrete Structures, Statistics) plus low-level systems, theory, and large programming projects. Performing in those while physically exhausted from twice-daily practices required sustained focus. Outcome: multiple Dean's List semesters, 3.301 GPA despite the compounding constraints.

## Lessons Learned
Every one of these resolved the same way: diagnose the real constraint, engineer around it, and own the result. Calculus II is the cleanest emblem — F to A, no excuses.
